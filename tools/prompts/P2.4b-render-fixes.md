Fix five things in `render.js`. The palette, material motifs, wobble, outlines, cracks
and effects are all good and must not be disturbed — this is targeted work. A reference
frame is at `shots/p2-render.png`; regenerate it after the changes and look at it.

## 1. The camera fits by width, and it must fit by height

Currently `makeCamera` frames the whole `viewMinX..viewMaxX` span, 44 world units. At
16:9 that forces about 25 world units of vertical extent, so the fortress occupies
roughly 15% of the frame and about 70% of the screen is empty sky. Everything is too
small to read and the ground is a featureless brown slab across the bottom fifth.

Fit by **height** instead and pan horizontally, which is what the genre does:

- The camera holds a `viewH` of about **13 world units** by default. Horizontal extent
  falls out of the aspect ratio, so a 16:9 screen sees roughly 23 world units.
- The ground line sits at about **78%** down the frame, not the middle. Sky above,
  a shallow band of earth below.
- Add `frameRect(camera, x0, y0, x1, y1, pad)` to fit an arbitrary world rectangle,
  clamping zoom so nothing ever gets smaller than the default. `game.js` will use it to
  pan between the sling and the fortress and to follow a critter in flight.
- Add `panTo(camera, x, y, t)` doing a smooth exponential approach, and clamp the camera
  so it never shows below the ground or outside `viewMinX..viewMaxX` horizontally.
- `makeCamera()` should default to framing the **fortress plot**, `x` from 0 to
  `TUNE.plotW`, since that is what the player looks at most.

## 2. There is no slingshot

`render.js` does not contain the string "sling". This is a slingshot game.

Draw it at `(TUNE.slingX, TUNE.slingY)` in the same inked storybook style as everything
else: a Y-shaped forked wooden frame, roughly 2.6 world units tall, wood colours, ink
outline, sitting on a small mound. Then:

- While the phase is `aiming` and the player is dragging, draw the two elastic bands
  from each fork tip to the held critter, as thick tapering dark-leather strokes, with
  the critter drawn between them and the near band drawn *over* the critter so it reads
  as held rather than floating.
- Draw the pouch.
- Draw a **predicted trajectory** as 8 to 12 fading dots along the launch parabola,
  spaced by time, not by distance, so the spacing itself communicates speed. Take the
  launch velocity the same way `launch()` in `sim.js` derives it so the preview cannot
  disagree with the shot.
- After release, leave the bands slack for about 0.3 s and draw a fading trail of the
  critter's recent positions.

Export `drawSlingshot(r, camera, round, aim)` if it is cleaner, but `draw` must render
it as part of a normal frame.

## 3. The ground is a flat slab

Give it the darker lip along the top edge that the original spec asked for, about 0.18
world units thick, plus stable scattered detail — tufts, small stones, a few dashes —
seeded from a fixed value so it is identical every run. It should read as ground, not
as a fill rectangle.

## 4. Pigs are not legible or distinguishable

At playable size a pig currently reads as a small pink dot, and the pig types cannot be
told apart, which matters because a player has to decide what to aim at.

- Give every pig a clear inked face: eyes with highlights, a snout with two nostrils,
  ears. Big, simple, high contrast.
- **The King Hog must be unmistakable at a glance** — plum body plus a brass crown with
  visible points, drawn above the head and not merged into the outline. Right now it
  cannot be picked out of a lineup.
- Distinguish the variants visibly: `helm` gets a metal helmet band across the top,
  `tusk` gets two cream tusks and a darker armoured cheek on the sling-facing side,
  `zep` gets a balloon above it on a short tether with the balloon as a separate drawn
  shape, `sarge` gets a cap, `hogg` reads bigger and heavier.

## 5. Iron reads as out of palette

The iron fill is noticeably cooler and darker than everything around it and it breaks
the warm scheme. Warm it slightly and lift it so it sits with the other materials while
staying obviously metal.

## Constraints

Do not change the palette values for anything other than iron. Do not disturb the
wobble, crack, shadow or effect systems. `render.js` may grow to 1300 lines; update its
budget in `docs/FILE-PLAN.json` if you exceed 1100, with a one-line reason.

## When done

Run `node --check render.js` and `node tools/check.mjs`. Then regenerate
`shots/p2-render.png` with a script at `tools/frame-shot.mjs` that boots a headless
Chromium against a local server, builds a representative fortress containing every
material and every pig type plus a damaged section, draws one frame at 1280×720, and
writes the PNG. Keep that script in the repository — being able to look at a frame
without launching the game is worth having permanently.

Report what you changed and confirm the frame now reads correctly.
