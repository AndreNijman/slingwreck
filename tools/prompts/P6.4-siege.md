## The siege phase

Both players attack the opponent's fortress **at the same time**, each simulating locally.
The relay does not simulate in real time; it audits.

Clients send:

- `shot` — `{ step, ammoIndex, dx, dy }` when they fire
- `tap` — `{ step }` when they trigger an ability
- `preview` — a lossy frame at `TUNE.previewHz` (8 Hz)
- `score` — their running score with each shot

The relay:

- appends shots and taps to that player's **shot log**, which is the audit record and the
  reconnect record
- relays `preview` straight through to the opponent, unmodified, at most 8 Hz per player.
  Drop frames rather than queueing them — a late preview frame is worthless, and §5 says
  nothing depends on it.
- ends the round when a King pops, both bags are spent and both worlds have settled, or the
  3-minute clock expires, per `docs/DESIGN.md` §6.3

**A King pop is claimed by a client and must be audited before it is believed**, because it
ends the round instantly and is therefore the single most valuable thing to lie about.
Verify it by replay before awarding, and if the replay disagrees, forfeit the claimant.

## The audit — this is the point of the phase

The relay replays each reported shot through the same `sim.js` and compares a score digest
at each shot boundary.

- Replay **incrementally, spread across real time** as shots arrive. Do not replay a whole
  round in one go: a Durable Object has a CPU budget and a 3-minute round is 10,800 steps
  per player. Keep a step budget per tick and catch up between shots.
- Compare `digestRound`, not just the score. A matching score with a different world means
  something is wrong even if the number looks right.
- On divergence beyond tolerance: forfeit the round to the opponent, log it, and tell both
  players plainly. Do not silently continue.
- Check unconditionally, even if the digest path is disabled: shots cannot arrive faster
  than the simulation allows, and a score cannot exceed the theoretical maximum for that
  fortress. Those two are cheap and catch the crude attacks.

Support `VALIDATE=lenient` as an env switch that keeps the bounds and timing checks and
drops the replay, per §5's fallback. It should not be needed — determinism is proven — but
the switch is the documented escape hatch and it must exist and be tested.

## Reconnect

A round is reconstructible from `(blueprint, seed, shot log)`, all of which the relay
holds. A reconnecting client identifies itself, receives those three, fast-forwards
locally, and rejoins. There is a 20-second grace before the round is awarded to the
connected player, per §6.3.

## tools/mp-smoke.mjs

Two real browsers against a real relay, driven through a whole round:

1. both connect, one creates a room with a password, the other joins
2. both build a fortress and lock in
3. assert each received the **other's authored blueprint**, and that settling it locally
   produces identical digests on both clients — this is the property §5 rests on and it
   must be asserted, not assumed
4. both siege simultaneously; assert previews arrive on both sides
5. one pops the enemy King; assert both clients see the round end within 500 ms and agree
   who won
6. one client drops and reconnects; assert it resumes with the same world

Fail on any console error, page error or failed request, as the other browser gates do.
Support `BASE_URL` and `LIVE_RELAY` so the same test can run against production.

## tools/audit-test.mjs

A deliberately lying client. At minimum:

- reports a score higher than its shots can produce
- claims a King pop that did not happen
- sends shots faster than the simulation allows

Each must be **caught and forfeited**, and the test must assert the honest client wins.
Also assert an honest client is never falsely accused across a full round — a false
positive here bans real players, which is worse than missing a cheat.
