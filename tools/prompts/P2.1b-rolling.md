Add **rolling resistance** to the contact solver in `physics.js`. This is a focused
bug fix with measured acceptance criteria. Read `ARCHITECTURE.md` §3 first — the
determinism ban list is absolute and `tools/check.mjs` enforces it.

## The bug, measured

A circle resting on flat ground rolls at a **constant speed forever**. Friction
converts sliding into rolling and then has nothing left to remove, because the solver
has no rolling resistance. Observed directly: a critter at 7.0000 units per second
was still at exactly 7.0000 eighty steps later.

The consequence is not cosmetic. `isSettled` is a **game rule** — it ends a round,
gates the next shot and locks a score — so nothing ever settles naturally and every
shot burns the full `TUNE.settleTimeout` of 6 seconds before the player may fire
again. Measured today, with the current build:

| scenario | time to return to `aiming` |
| --- | --- |
| miss onto empty ground | 6.02 s |
| hit a small tower | 6.00 s |

Both are the timeout firing, not the world settling.

## What not to do

Global angular damping was tried and rejected. It is already in `TUNE` at `0.4` for
stability and should stay small. Raising it does work, eventually, but it is the wrong
lever and the numbers show why:

| `angularDamping` | miss | tower | knock a pig off the plot edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 | 6.02 s | 6.00 s | succeeds |
| 2.0 | 6.02 s | 6.00 s | **fails** |
| 4.0 | 4.92 s | 5.78 s | **fails** |
| 8.0 | 3.03 s | 3.78 s | **fails** |

It is still too slow at a value high enough to visibly drain spin from a critter in
mid-flight, and it removes the energy that carries a pig off the edge of the plot.
Damping cannot tell the difference between a body resting on the ground and a body in
the air, and that difference is the entire point.

## What to do

Implement rolling resistance **at the contact**, in the velocity solver, so it only
acts while something is actually resting on something else and free flight is
untouched.

- Add `rollingFriction` to `TUNE` in `data.js` and tune it to meet the criteria below.
  Consider whether it belongs per material as well; if it does, add it to `MATERIALS`
  with a one-line justification per material, and combine two bodies' values the same
  way friction is combined.
- In the velocity solver, alongside the existing normal and tangent impulses, apply an
  **angular impulse opposing the relative angular velocity of the pair**, magnitude
  clamped to `rollingFriction * pn * effectiveRadius`, accumulated and clamped the same
  way the normal and tangent impulses already are. Clamping the accumulator rather than
  the increment matters here for the same reason it matters for the normal impulse.
- It must scale with the accumulated normal impulse `pn`, so a lightly touching body is
  barely slowed and a heavily loaded one resists strongly. A constant torque would stop
  a rolling ball in mid-air the instant it brushed a wall.

## Acceptance criteria — all must hold

1. Miss onto empty ground returns to `aiming` in **under 2.0 s**.
2. Hit a small tower returns to `aiming` in **under 2.5 s**.
3. A critter fired flat at a pig sitting near the right-hand edge of the plot still
   knocks it off and the round reaches `won`. This is the criterion that global damping
   broke; it is here to stop the fix overshooting.
4. All seven assertions in `node tools/physics-test.mjs` still pass, and the pyramid's
   `max move` stays under 0.05.
5. All eight assertions in `node tools/sim-test.mjs` still pass.
6. `node tools/check.mjs` passes.
7. `SKIP_BROWSERS=1 node tools/determinism-test.mjs` passes. Digests will change, which
   is expected; they must simply still be self-consistent.

Reproduce criteria 1 to 3 with a script you write at `tools/settle-probe.mjs`, keep it
in the repository, print measured seconds next to the thresholds, and exit non-zero on
failure. Add it to the suites list in `tools/check.mjs` so it cannot rot.

## Style

2-space indent, single quotes, semicolons. Comments explain *why* and name the
alternative that failed — the angular-damping table above is exactly the kind of thing
worth one sentence in the code.
