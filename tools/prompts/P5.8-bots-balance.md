## bots.js

Two jobs, per `docs/FILE-PLAN.json`: fortress templates for solo Siege later, and **a
ballistic aimer** which is what this task needs.

The aimer, given a round and a target point, solves the launch vector that hits it. It is
a closed-form projectile solve against `TUNE.gravity` from the sling anchor, clamped to
`TUNE.slingRadius`, with a difficulty parameter adding deterministic noise. **Seeded noise
only** — take an `rng` and thread it, because a bot run has to be reproducible or its
output cannot be used to set thresholds.

Target selection needs to be better than "aim at a pig", since no pig is directly hittable
by design. Aim at **structural weak points**: the lowest load-bearing block under the
largest mass, a block whose removal drops something onto a pig, an exposed TNT crate. A
short ranked list of candidate targets, scored, and the bot takes the best. It does not
have to be clever, it has to be consistent — its job is to establish what a competent
player can achieve, not to play well.

`bots.js` must obey the determinism ban: no `Math.random`, no wall clock. It runs headless
and it will run inside the relay later.

## tools/balance.mjs --campaign

For every one of the 52 levels: play it with the bot across several seeds, and report

- whether it was completed, and with how many critters left
- the score achieved, best and median across seeds
- shots taken
- which pigs survived on a failure

**Exit non-zero** when any level is unwinnable by the bot, since `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md`
requires every level be completable with at least one critter to spare.

## Then set the stars

Star thresholds have been deliberately `null` since P5.1 precisely so they could be
derived from play rather than invented. Derive them:

- **one star** — completing the level at all
- **two stars** — a competent clear, around the bot's median score
- **three stars** — better than the bot's best, so it rewards a human doing something the
  bot did not find

Write them into `levels.js` via the existing `--import`-style rewrite, and print the table.
State the formula you used in a comment in `levels.js` so the next person knows these are
derived numbers with a provenance, not taste.

Report any level where the bot's best and median are nearly identical: that means the level
has one solution and no room for mastery, which is worth knowing even if it is not fixed
now.
