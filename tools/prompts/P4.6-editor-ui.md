Build the fortress editor's interface. Read `build.js` (all the logic exists — do not
duplicate or reimplement any of it), `game.js`, `render.js`, `style.css`,
`docs/DESIGN.md` §6.2, and `shots/p2-title.png` plus `shots/p3-hulk.png` for the visual
register.

## Why this one matters more than it looks

The 52 campaign levels in P5 are authored **in this editor** and exported from it. That
is deliberate: it is the only way the editor gets enough use to become good. So it is not
a Siege sub-screen that can be rough — it is the tool the rest of the project is built
with, and every awkwardness in it gets paid for 52 times.

## The screen

A new editor screen, reachable from the title screen, alongside the existing play flow.
It renders the plot through the existing `render.js` camera framed on the plot, with the
slingshot visible for reference so the builder can see what they are defending against.

### Palette

Grouped: **materials** (each with its shapes) and **pigs**. Every entry shows its
**cost**, drawn with the real renderer rather than described in text — a small canvas
thumbnail per entry, so the palette looks like the game. Locked materials are visibly
locked with the card that unlocks them named.

Selected entry is obvious. Number keys 1–9 select within the current group, `Tab`
switches group.

### Placing

- Hover shows a **ghost** of the piece at the snapped position, drawn at partial alpha,
  tinted to say whether the placement is legal — it must be immediately obvious that a
  piece would overlap, leave the plot, or cost more than is left.
- Click places. **Drag sweeps a run** of the same piece along the drag, snapped, which is
  the single biggest time saver when building a wall and the thing that makes authoring
  52 levels bearable.
- Right-click or `X` removes the piece under the cursor.
- `R` rotates the selection in 15° steps. There is no free rotation — see
  `docs/DESIGN.md` §10.
- `G` toggles a faint grid overlay. Draw it in the ink colour at low alpha, not as
  bright lines; it must not compete with the fortress.
- `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo through `build.js`.
- Clicking an existing piece picks it up and re-places it.

### Budget meter

Remaining scrap, prominent, in the storybook register. It must show the **cost of the
currently hovered placement** before committing — "you have 12 left, this costs 5" is
the decision the builder is making, and making them do that arithmetic is bad design.
Turn it red when the hovered piece is unaffordable.

### Validation

Run `validate` continuously and show the result as a short list of human-readable
problems. Clicking a problem **highlights the offending pieces** in the world — `build.js`
already returns `pieceIds` for every error, precisely so this is possible. Do not just
print a code.

The King rule, the two-pig rule and burial depth all need to read as guidance rather than
scolding: "your King is too deeply buried — there must be a way in within 5 blocks" is
useful; `buried-king` is not.

### Settle test

A button that runs `settleTest` and **plays the three seconds on screen** so the builder
watches their tower settle. On failure, highlight what moved and which pigs died, and
leave the draft untouched so they can fix it. This is the affordance that stops a
collapse being a surprise at lock-in.

### Export, for authoring levels

A copy-to-clipboard of the encoded blueprint string, and a paste-to-load. That is how
campaign levels will get out of this editor and into `levels.js` in P5, so it must exist
now and it must round-trip exactly.

## Style

Follow the existing `style.css` register — warm parchment surfaces, ink text, chunky
rounded controls with a visible press state, system font stack, nothing pure black or
white. The palette is the only new large piece of chrome; keep it out of the plot area,
and make sure the plot stays fully visible on a phone in portrait, since that is most of
the traffic.

Real `<button>` elements, labels on controls, visible focus, `aria-live` on the
validation summary. Keyboard reachable throughout.

## Verify by looking

Add `tools/editor-shots.mjs` that drives the real editor in headless Chromium and
captures, at 1280×720 and again at 390×844 portrait:

- `shots/p4-editor-empty.png` — the editor as first opened
- `shots/p4-editor-building.png` — mid-build with a ghost shown and the budget partly spent
- `shots/p4-editor-invalid.png` — a blueprint with two validation problems, highlighted
- `shots/p4-editor-settle.png` — a settle test failing, with the moved pieces marked

Then **look at all four** and report what you see. Three defects in P2 — a camera that
never showed the fortress, illegible pigs, a slingshot that was never drawn — were
invisible in every written report and obvious in the first screenshot. Do not report that
the editor looks correct without having looked at it.

## Acceptance

1. `node tools/editor-shots.mjs` writes all four frames at both sizes.
2. `node tools/check.mjs`, `node tools/editor-test.mjs`, `npm test`,
   `npm run test:audio` all pass. Zero console errors, page errors or failed requests.
3. Extend `tools/smoke.mjs`: open the editor, place pieces, hit an invalid state, fix it,
   run a settle test, export and re-import the blueprint, and assert the draft matches.
   The export round trip is the one P5 depends on.
4. Report the portrait behaviour honestly. If the palette and the plot genuinely cannot
   coexist at 390 px wide, say so and describe what you did instead.

## Budget

`game.js` may grow to 1800 lines; if the editor pushes past that, split it into
`editor-ui.js` and add it to `docs/FILE-PLAN.json` with a reason rather than compressing.
`render.js` may grow by 150 lines for the ghost, grid and highlight drawing.
