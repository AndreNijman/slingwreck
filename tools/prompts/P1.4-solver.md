## Non-negotiables

1. **Only `Math.sqrt`, `abs`, `min`, `max`, `floor`, `ceil`, `round`, `sign`.** No
   trigonometry, no `pow`, no `hypot`, no `random`. `tools/check.mjs` fails the build
   on any other `Math.*`.
2. **Deterministic iteration order everywhere.** Bodies in ascending `id`. Contacts in
   generation order. You may use a `Map` for the warm-start cache because JavaScript
   `Map` iteration is insertion-ordered by specification, but **iterate the contacts
   array, never the Map**. No sorting by a float key unless `id` breaks the tie.
3. Re-run `npm run test:determinism` when you are done. The core already proves four
   engines agree; the solver must not break that.

## The solver

### Broadphase
Sort-and-sweep on the x axis over AABBs. 120 bodies does not need a BVH, and a tree
would add a rebuild order that has to be made deterministic for no gain. Compute
AABBs from the rotated vertices (or centre ± r for circles). Skip pairs where both
bodies are static, both are asleep, or both are dead.

### Narrowphase
- **polygon-polygon:** SAT over both bodies' face normals. Pick the axis of least
  penetration as the reference face, clip the incident face against the reference
  face's two side planes (Sutherland–Hodgman), keep points still behind the reference
  face. Up to **two** contact points.
- **circle-polygon:** closest point on the polygon to the centre. One contact point.
  Handle the centre-inside-polygon case, or a circle that gets swallowed by a fast
  block will be ejected in a random direction.
- **circle-circle:** one contact point. Handle exactly coincident centres by picking a
  fixed axis rather than dividing by a zero distance.

Two contact points for face-face contact is not optional. **One point per pair and a
plank resting on two posts see-saws forever**, which looks like a physics bug to a
player and is actually a manifold bug.

### Contact identity and warm starting
Give each contact point a stable integer key from `(bodyA.id, bodyB.id, featureId)`
where `featureId` encodes which reference face and which incident vertex produced it.
Always order the pair so the lower `id` is A, or the key flips between frames and
warm starting silently does nothing. Cache the accumulated normal and tangent
impulses under that key and seed the next frame with them. Drop cache entries not
touched this frame.

Warm starting is what makes a ten-high stack settle in a few frames instead of
visibly sinking and shivering. Without it the rest of the tuning cannot be judged.

### Velocity solver
`TUNE.velocityIters` (8) sequential iterations. Per contact point:

- Normal impulse with **accumulated-impulse clamping**: keep a running `pn` per point,
  compute the increment, clamp so `pn` never goes below zero, apply the *difference*.
  Clamping the increment instead of the accumulator is the classic mistake and it
  makes stacks spongy.
- Coulomb friction with `mu = sqrt(a.fric * b.fric)`, clamped against the accumulated
  normal impulse of that same point, applied the same accumulated way.
- **Restitution is a velocity bias computed once when the manifold is built**, from
  the approach velocity along the normal, and applied every iteration. Use
  `rest = max(a.rest, b.rest)`, and apply **no restitution at all** when the approach
  speed is below a threshold (`1.0` world units per second is a good starting point,
  put it in `TUNE` as `restitutionThreshold`). Resting contacts must not bounce, and
  restitution computed fresh each iteration pumps energy into the stack.

### Position correction — read this carefully
**Do not put Baumgarte bias in the velocity solver.** Do positional correction only,
in the position pass, as `TUNE.positionIters` (3) iterations of non-linear
Gauss-Seidel: recompute penetration from current positions, and move bodies apart by
`baumgarte * max(0, penetration - slop)` split by inverse mass, adjusting rotation
from the contact lever arm.

Both together double-correct, and the velocity-bias half of it injects energy that
has nowhere to go, which is the single most common reason a hand-written solver
cannot stack more than four boxes. `TUNE.baumgarte` is the position pass's correction
fraction, nothing else. Say so in a comment.

### Islands and sleeping
Union-find over contact pairs. **Static bodies must not join islands** — the ground
touches everything, so linking through statics merges the whole world into one island
that can never sleep. An island sleeps when every dynamic body in it has been under
`sleepLinear` and `sleepAngular` for `sleepTime`. Sleeping zeroes velocities. Any new
contact with an awake body, or any external impulse, wakes the whole island.

Sleeping is a **game rule** here, not an optimisation: "the world has settled" ends a
siege round, validates a blueprint and locks a score. Export
`isSettled(world)` returning true when every dynamic body is asleep or dead.

### Raycast
`raycast(world, x0, y0, x1, y1, filter)` returning the nearest hit as
`{ body, t, x, y, nx, ny }` or `null`. Needed for explosion occlusion and for the
build phase's burial-depth rule. Slab test against AABBs first, then exact
segment-versus-polygon and segment-versus-circle. `filter(body)` returning false skips
a body. Also export `raycastAll` returning every hit sorted by `t`, which the burial
rule needs to count distinct blocks along a ray.

## tools/physics-test.mjs

The seven assertions from `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md` P1, each printed as a named PASS or
FAIL line with the measured number next to the threshold, exiting non-zero on any
failure. Restated:

1. A 10-high stack of `cube` bodies is asleep within 2 s and has drifted less than
   0.02 world units from its starting x.
2. A pyramid of `plank` and `post` bodies stands for 30 s and is still asleep at the
   end.
3. A dropped `ball`'s successive bounce heights follow the restitution coefficient
   within 5%.
4. A box on a 20° ramp does not slide with `mu 0.6` and does slide with `mu 0.2`.
   Build the ramp as a rotated static box using `fromDegrees`.
5. 200 bodies dropped into an open box reach sleep within 6 s and none escape the
   container.
6. Reversing gravity from a settled state produces no `NaN` in any body field.
7. Two independently constructed identical worlds run 3600 steps and digest equal.

Print measurements, not just verdicts — `stack drift 0.004 < 0.02` is debuggable and
`PASS` alone is not.
