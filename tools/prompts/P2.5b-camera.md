Fix the camera and the slingshot placement. Everything else about the slice is good —
the title screen, HUD, input, accumulator, level and result panel all work and must not
be disturbed. Inspection frames are in `shots/`: `p2-title.png`, `p2-drawn.png`,
`p2-after.png`.

## What is wrong, from the frames

In `p2-drawn.png` the player is at full draw and **the fortress is entirely off-screen**
— one plank edge at the extreme right. They are aiming at something they cannot see.
About 85% of the frame is empty hills.

In `p2-after.png` the shot has landed and settled, a critter has been consumed, and the
camera is **still parked at the slingshot**. It never returns to the fortress, so the
player never sees what their shot did. That is the whole feedback loop of the genre and
it is currently missing.

## The root cause

`TUNE.slingX` is `-16` and the plot runs `0` to `24`, so the scene spans over 40 world
units. The camera fits by height at 13.5 units, giving about 24 units of width on 16:9.
Sling and fortress cannot both be on screen at any zoom the camera currently permits,
and the note in the last handoff — that the minimum zoom cannot show both — was the
symptom, not something to work around.

## The fix

### Move the slingshot closer

Set `TUNE.slingX` to `-9` in `data.js`, and tighten `TUNE.viewMinX` to `-13`. A 33-unit
throw from the sling to the far edge of the plot is still comfortably inside the
`launchSpeedMax` of 30 against gravity 22, and it halves the dead ground that currently
does nothing but push the fortress out of frame. Re-run `tools/sim-test.mjs` afterwards;
the reach assertion should still pass, and if it does not, say so rather than adjusting
the assertion.

### Let the aiming camera zoom out

The instruction to clamp zoom so nothing ever gets smaller than the default was wrong
and it is what wedged this. Replace it with an explicit minimum zoom that is generous
enough to fit the whole playfield, and use it:

- **aiming** — frame `[TUNE.slingX - 3, TUNE.plotW + 2]` horizontally with the ground
  low in the frame, so the sling and the entire fortress are both visible. Objects are
  smaller here and that is correct; this is the planning view.
- **flying** — pan and zoom *in* to the default 13.5-unit height, following the critter
  and leading it slightly. This is the exciting view and the zoom change between the
  two states is itself good feedback.
- **settling** — frame the fortress plot and hold there long enough to actually watch
  the collapse, then return to the aiming framing when the phase goes back to `aiming`.

All transitions through `panTo`, never a cut.

### Let the player look around

Dragging on empty background must pan the camera horizontally, clamped to the
playfield, with a spring back to the automatic framing when released. Dragging from the
pouch still aims. Distinguish them by where the press starts: inside the pouch grab
radius it aims, otherwise it pans. This is how the genre lets a player study a fortress
before committing, and without it a level bigger than one screen cannot be read.

Keep the existing wheel and pinch zoom, layered on top.
