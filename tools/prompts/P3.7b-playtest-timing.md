Refine `tools/playtest.mjs`. The tool works and its failure mode works; the measurement
is confounded and is producing false alarms.

## What the last run actually showed

Three "tapped is worse than untapped" findings were reported as probable bugs. All three
are artifacts of the harness, and diagnosing them is the fix:

1. **`tapStep = 50` is a single fixed tap for every critter.** At 50 steps the critter is
   mid-flight, well short of the keep. A `lob` that detonates in open air is *supposed*
   to do less than one that is allowed to hit something. Tapping every ability at the
   same instant measures the instant, not the ability.
2. **Glass damage was `36.00` for both tapped and untapped `chip`.** The fixture contains
   one glass post and one glass plank — 12 plus 24 hp, exactly 36. The metric was
   **saturated**: both runs destroyed all the glass there was. "No improvement" was the
   ceiling, not a failure.
3. `zip`'s tapped and untapped rows differ only in wood damage and travel, which is the
   same timing problem as `lob`.

## Fix the measurement

### Sweep the tap, do not fix it

For each critter, run the tap at several points across the flight — a handful spread
between shortly after launch and just before impact — and report the **best** outcome by
score together with **which** tap step produced it. Keep the untapped run as the
baseline.

That gives two genuinely useful numbers instead of one misleading one: the ability's
ceiling, and how fussy its timing is. An ability that only works in a two-step window is
a design problem worth knowing about, and this is the only thing that would surface it.

Print the best row per critter in the main table. Add a `--sweep` flag that prints every
tap step for every critter for when someone is chasing one ability.

### Report damage as a fraction of what was there

For every material, print damage **and** the total hit points of that material present in
the fixture, or the percentage. A saturated metric must be visibly saturated —
`36.0 / 36.0 (100%)` says "ceiling reached", where `36.00` says "no effect", and those
are opposite conclusions.

### Give the fixture enough of everything

Raise the amounts so nothing saturates in a single shot: more glass, more stone, and make
sure `tnt` and `sand` are actually reachable, since the last run left both untouched and
therefore untested. Keep one fixture shared by all critters so rows stay comparable.

## Then re-read it

With the sweep in place, the gate criterion becomes the honest one:

> an ability fails if **no** tap step anywhere in the sweep beats the untapped baseline.

That is the real bug — an ability with no timing at which it helps. Implement that as the
failure condition, replacing "tapped and untapped telemetry are identical".

Report the new table and say, per critter, whether it is genuinely working. Be specific
about `lob`, `zip` and `chip`, since those are the three currently under suspicion. If
any of them is still worse at every tap step, that is a real bug — say so plainly and do
**not** adjust numbers to hide it; flag it for `tools/balance.mjs`.
