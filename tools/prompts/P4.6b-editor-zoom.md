One fix to the editor: the plot is rendered too small to build in. Everything else —
the palette, the costs, the locked-card labels, the validation wording, the blueprint
string, the settle test — is good and must not change.

## The measurement

From `shots/p4-editor-building.png` and `shots/p4-editor-invalid.png` at 1280×720:

The editor camera frames the slingshot at x −9 through the far edge of the plot at x 24.
That is 33 world units across roughly 715 pixels of canvas, so about **21 px per world
unit**. A 1×1 cube is 21 px. In the invalid frame, the two overlapping blocks the
validator is pointing at are a red smudge about 30 px wide — you cannot see what is
wrong with them.

The slingshot and its approach take up the left quarter of the view and it is empty
ground. Meanwhile the fortress, which is the thing being edited, gets the rest.

For comparison, gameplay renders a cube at roughly 55 px. Authoring 52 levels at 21 px
per unit means every placement is fiddly and every mistake is hard to see.

## The fix

**Default the editor camera to the plot, not the plot plus the slingshot.** Frame x from
about −1 to `TUNE.plotW + 1`, which is 26 units across the same 715 px — roughly **28 px
per unit**, a third larger — and let the vertical framing use the space it has rather
than being width-locked by dead ground.

**Keep the slingshot legible without paying for it.** Draw it as a compact marker
clamped to the left edge of the view: the small Y-fork icon, an arrow showing the firing
direction, and the distance in world units. The builder needs to know where the shots
come from and how far away it is; they do not need to look at empty ground to know that.

Keep the existing wheel and pinch zoom and the drag-to-pan, so anyone who does want to
see the real sling can pan to it.

## Also

The highlight for a validation error needs to survive being small. Give it an outline
plus a pulse or a leader line, not just a red tint — at any zoom, clicking "these pieces
overlap" must make it obvious *which* pieces, and a tint on a 30 px block does not.
