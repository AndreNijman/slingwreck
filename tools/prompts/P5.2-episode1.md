## What the existing three levels get wrong

`sty-01`, `sty-02` and `sty-03` all validate, settle and look tidy. They are still weak
levels, and the contact sheet is what makes that obvious:

1. **They are flat.** All three use roughly 3 of the 16 available vertical world units.
   The appeal of this genre is toppling something tall; a wide low row of blocks is the
   least interesting shape a fortress can be.
2. **There is no visible escalation.** `sty-03` has 15 pieces against `sty-02`'s 10 and
   reads as *easier* — it is wider, not harder. Piece count is not difficulty.
3. **Both 02 and 03 have a detached lump on the right** that serves no purpose. It looks
   like motif composition filling space rather than a designed target.

Keep the three but rebuild them to the direction below; they are numbers 1 to 3 and they
carry the most teaching load.

## Direction

**Escalate vertically.** Level 1 is a low hut, roughly 2–3 units tall. Level 13 is a
genuine tower, 8–10 units. The silhouette should change visibly across the episode, and
the contact sheet should read as a progression when you look down it.

**One idea per level.** Each level should have a single clear solution the player can
see: *this support is load-bearing*, *this glass pane is the way in*, *this thing will
topple to the right if hit high*. State the idea in a comment above each level. If you
cannot name it in one sentence, the level does not have one yet.

**A second structure must earn its place.** Only include one when it forces a *different*
shot — a target needing a flatter trajectory, or one only reachable after the first has
fallen. Never as filler.

**Difficulty comes from the shape, not the volume.** A three-piece level with a
load-bearing post placed awkwardly is harder than a fifteen-piece row. Watch the ratio of
bag size to pieces: as the episode progresses, give proportionally *fewer* critters.

## Teaching order

Episode 1 unlocks `nib`, `chip`, `wedge`, and the Runt and Swine, over wood and glass
only. No stone, no TNT, nothing later.

| levels | job |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 | `nib` only. Aiming, arc, and that supports matter more than roofs |
| 4–6 | introduce `chip`. Glass-heavy fronts where splitting beats a single hit |
| 7–9 | introduce `wedge`. A wood column or a stack of planks that wants drilling through |
| 10–12 | combine all three; the player chooses which critter solves which part |
| 13 | the episode's biggest structure — tall, multi-stage, every critter needed |

Bags are hand-picked per level from those three, in a deliberate order. The order is part
of the puzzle: `chip, nib, wedge` plays differently from `wedge, chip, nib`.

## Constraints

- Campaign rule set: inside the plot, nothing overlapping, at most 120 blocks. No King, no
  budget, no burial-depth rule.
- **Every level must pass `settleTest`** with movement under the strict tolerance.
- Star thresholds stay `null`. They are set in P5.8 from bot play. Do not invent them.
- Compose from `motifs.js` where a motif fits, and place pieces directly where it does
  not — a motif that has to be contorted to fit a level idea is the wrong tool, and
  bending the level to fit the motif is worse.
