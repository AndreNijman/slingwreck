Fix the grid, and then put `settleTest` back to being strict. The seating tolerance you
added is treating a cause as a fact of life, and it is hiding the thing it was added to
work around.

## The measurement

`settleTest` now allows a pig to move `TUNE.gridSnap + tolerance`, which is **0.55 world
units** — more than a full cube edge. For comparison, a correctly built three-tier
pyramid in `tools/physics-test.mjs` moves **0.021**, and 0.54 of movement was what
identified a *collapsing* structure back in P1. A tolerance that large cannot tell
seating from collapse, which is the one job it has.

The cause is not pigs being awkward. It is that **the grid cannot express where things
rest.**

A body rests with its bottom at `y = 0` when its centre is at `y = halfHeight`. The
shapes need these centre heights:

| shape | half-height |
| --- | --- |
| `beam`, `plank` | **0.25** |
| `cube`, `slab`, `ball` | 0.5 |
| `post` | 1 |
| `pillar` | 2 |

`TUNE.gridSnap` is `0.5`, so **0.25 is not reachable** — a plank on the ground either
floats a quarter unit or sinks a quarter unit into it. And no pig radius aligns to any
grid at all:

```
runt  0.30 (off by 0.20)   swine 0.40 (0.10)   hogg  0.58 (-0.08)   helm  0.42 (0.08)
tusk  0.44 (0.06)          zep   0.34 (0.16)   sarge 0.46 (0.04)    king  0.68 (-0.18)
```

Every pig in the game is misplaced by between 0.04 and 0.20 units before the first step
runs. The 0.205 you measured is a Runt falling 0.2 to reach the ground.

## Two fixes

### 1. `TUNE.gridSnap` becomes `0.25`

Then every block half-height — 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 — lands exactly, and a plank placed on the
ground rests on the ground.

Consequences to handle:

- The codec stores positions as `x * 2`; it becomes `x * 4`. The plot is 24 wide, so 96
  steps horizontally and 64 vertically, which still packs small. **Report the new average
  encoded size.**
- Bump the codec version and keep decoding the old one, as you already do.
- At the editor's 27 px per unit, a 0.25 step is about 7 px, which is fine for snapping.
  If it feels too fine while dragging, keep a coarse 0.5 modifier — but the default has
  to be able to express a resting plank.

### 2. Pigs seat themselves vertically

A pig's radius will never align to any sensible grid, and making a builder hand-tune `y`
per pig type would be miserable design even if it worked. So: a pig placement snaps `x`
to the grid and **computes `y` so the pig rests exactly on whatever surface is beneath
it** — ground, or the top of a block.

Do it deterministically with a downward `raycast` from the placement point, seat the pig
at `hit.y + radius`, and store that exact `y` in the blueprint. The codec must be able to
carry it, so if a seated `y` is not on the grid, the pig tuple needs its `y` at a finer
quantisation than blocks — say 1/64 of a unit, which is exact enough that the residual
movement is far below the solver's own slop.

Report what you chose and what the residual movement becomes.

## Then restore the strictness

With both fixes in, delete the seating tolerances and put `settleTest` back to a single
tight tolerance. Then assert it: **every one of the three example levels must settle with
`maxMovement` under 0.02**, matching the standard the physics gates are held to.

If some piece still cannot make that, do not widen the tolerance — say which piece and
why, and we will fix the placement. Widening a tolerance to fit a measurement is the
first rule in `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md` and it is there because it has already happened once
on this project.
