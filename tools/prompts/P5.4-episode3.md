## What this episode adds

Per `EPISODES` in `data.js`: `pebble` and `boomer` join the bag; Tusker and Zeppelin Hog
join the pigs; **spring pads** join the materials. Everything from Episodes 1 and 2
carries over. Nothing from Episode 4.

Note on the theme: `EPISODES` says "towers, spring pads, wind". **There is no wind in the
campaign** — `gale` is a Siege card and nothing in `sim.js` applies wind to a campaign
level. Do not invent it. The episode's identity is height and bounce instead, which is
what spring pads and the two new critters are for.

Teaching order:

| levels | job |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 | `pebble`. Tall front walls with the target behind them, so dropping a payload over the top is the only way in |
| 4–6 | `boomer`. Targets genuinely behind the fortress, reachable only by reversing past it |
| 7–9 | spring pads. Bounce a critter into somewhere a direct arc cannot reach, and at least one level where a pad protects the fortress instead of helping you |
| 10–12 | Tusker and Zeppelin Hog. The Tusker's armour faces the sling, so it must be hit from behind or above. The Zeppelin drifts — lead it, or drop its balloon and let the fall finish it |
| 13 | the tallest structure in the campaign so far, using height, a spring pad and both new pigs |

Specific to this episode:

- **Tusker armour is directional and faces the slingshot.** A level whose only answer is a
  frontal hit does not work. Verify by probe, as Episode 2 did for the Helmet Hog: a
  frontal shot must leave it alive, and the intended answer must kill it.
- **The Zeppelin drifts, so a level cannot depend on it being at one exact spot.** Any
  solution requiring it must work across its whole drift range, and remember the
  drift-extreme bounds rule.
- **Spring pads cut both ways.** At least one level should use a pad defensively, so the
  player learns to avoid hitting it.
